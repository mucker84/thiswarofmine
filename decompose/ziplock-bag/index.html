How Long Does a Ziplock Bag Take to Decompose?

1,000 years
multilayer plastic that barely breaks down

A ziplock bag takes about 1,000 years to decompose. Its convenience hides a stubborn environmental cost.

Why does it take this long?

Made from LDPE plastic with a multilayer, sealable structure, it resists breaking down and instead crumbles slowly into microplastics. The seal and thicker walls make it even more durable than a regular plastic bag — meaning it lasts, and pollutes, far longer.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a ziplock bag take to decompose?
A ziplock bag takes approximately 1,000 years to decompose in nature.
Can ziplock bags be recycled?
Some store drop-off programs accept clean LDPE bags, but they're not accepted in most curbside recycling.
Can I reuse ziplock bags?
Yes — washing and reusing them many times is the simplest way to cut their impact.

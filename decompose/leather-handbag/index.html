How Long Does a Leather Handbag Take to Decompose?

25–40 years
chemical tanning slows natural decay

A leather handbag takes 25 to 40 years to decompose. Leather is an animal product, so you might expect it to break down quickly — but industrial leather is heavily processed.

Why does it take this long?

Most leather is chrome-tanned, treated with chemicals that stop it from rotting (that's the whole point of tanning). Those same chemicals slow decomposition and can contaminate soil with heavy metals as the bag finally breaks down.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a leather handbag take to decompose?
A leather handbag takes approximately 25–40 years to decompose in nature.
Does leather biodegrade?
Vegetable-tanned leather breaks down reasonably well; chrome-tanned leather resists decay and can pollute soil.
Is leather eco-friendly?
It depends on tanning. Traditional veg-tanned leather is far greener than conventional chrome-tanned leather.

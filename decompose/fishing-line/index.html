How Long Does a Nylon Fishing Line Take to Decompose?

600 years
an invisible trap for fish and birds

Discarded nylon fishing line takes around 600 years to decompose. Thin, transparent, and incredibly strong, it becomes an invisible hazard the moment it's left behind.

Why does it take this long?

Birds, fish, and marine mammals get entangled and cannot free themselves. Because it's nearly invisible in water, lost line keeps trapping wildlife for centuries — a phenomenon known as "ghost fishing." Anglers who leave line on banks and in water create traps that outlast their great-great-grandchildren.
